Choosing which paintings you focus with

Easel’s paintings come from a hand-picked selection of over 600 public-domain works in The Met’s collection. You can let Easel surprise you, or focus with one style, artist or era.

Choose a collection

  1. On the home screen, tap the card under Focus with (it reads All paintings at first).
  2. Pick a collection. Each row shows how many of its works you’ve uncovered, like 22 / 90.
  3. Tap Done. Your next sessions draw only from that collection, until you change it.

By style

  • European Paintings: Rembrandt, Vermeer, Van Gogh, Cézanne, Corot, Courbet, Manet, Renoir, Degas and more.
  • Japanese Prints: Katsushika Hokusai, Utagawa Hiroshige and other Edo and Meiji masters.
  • American Paintings: Hudson River School landscapes by Kensett, Inness, Church and others.
  • Flowers & Birds: John James Audubon, Ogata Kōrin and botanical masters.

By artist and by era

Below the styles, the sheet lists artists with several works in Easel, and eras from the Renaissance to the Modern era (1880 onward). Choose one to spend a week with Hokusai, or a month in the nineteenth century.

How the paintings are chosen

Every work is in the public domain and comes from The Met’s Open Access collection. The selection favours celebrated works and is curated to be calm and suitable for everyone: Easel never picks a painting at random from the museum’s whole collection.
